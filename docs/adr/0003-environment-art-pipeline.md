# ADR 0003: Environment art pipeline (terrain, buildings, textures, lighting)

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Related: [ADR 0001](0001-engine-and-architecture.md) (text-first data, generated levels), [findings](../findings.md) (scale, texture sizes)

## Context

Today every zone is a generated blockout:
- `tools/roo2gltf` turns each `.roo` into one glTF mesh, with one primitive per original texture (material slots `grdNNNNN`) and UVs tiled to the original texel size.
- `tools/ue/build_world.py` imports that mesh into `/Game/Generated/Zones/Z<rid>`, uses it as complex collision, and puts it in the zone's streaming sublevel.
- `data/zone_layout.json` holds the Kod-placed objects (Raza alone has 63 `OrnamentalObject`s across about 12 `type`s, 9 lamps, 2 braziers and 2 signs). Nothing renders them yet.

The reference shots (`ReferenceImages/`) show the look we're starting from:
- Doom-style geometry: flat floors, vertical walls and a few slopes.
- Low-resolution textures (most are 64–128 px per 2.2 m square, about 30–60 px/m) with lighting painted into them.
- Sprite trees and props, and a strong, saturated palette (green grass, grey cobble, timber-and-plaster houses, purple night sky).

The target is **mid-poly fantasy that stays faithful to the original** (revised 2026-10-04, was "low-poly fantasy"). The original textures and facades stay the identity of the world. Geometry gets real depth where the originals only painted it: recessed windows, proud trims, solid merlons, grass. Modern lighting comes on top: normal maps, displacement, GI, fog, grading and VFX. The detail level should sit with the MakeHuman characters (ADR 0002). It does not mean photoreal, and it does not mean chunky stylized low-poly either.

### Constraints that shape the decision
1. **We may use the original art.** The project lead built the Server 103 and Server 104 websites and works closely with both teams. As of 2026-10-04 we have their permission to use **all original game assets**, on two conditions: the product uses a different name (it's *Meridian Remastered*, with no "104") and it runs on its own server (ADR 0001 §2). Both are already met. Upscaled, retouched or AI-reworked versions of the originals can ship. ADR 0001's "confirm before public release" note is now resolved.
   - **Repo hygiene is unchanged.** Raw extracted art stays out of git, and `tools/setup.ps1` rebuilds it from the installed client. Derived textures are regenerated by script where possible. Only hand-retouched final textures get committed (LFS, `art_src/`).
   - **Goal:** stay close to the original look. Keep the colours, motifs and the recognizable building facades, and add resolution, normals and modern lighting.
2. **Levels are regenerated, not hand-saved.** ADR 0001 §5 has editor scripts build levels from text data. Anything we hand-edit has to survive a re-run of `roo2gltf` and `build_world.py`.
3. **Gameplay depends on the blockout.** Floor heights, exits, spawn points and the zone smoke test all assume the `roo2gltf` geometry. Art must not break walkability.
4. **Small team, AI-assisted.** Prefer scripted, text-described steps over large one-off binary edits, and Blender over new paid tools. The user is already fluent in Blender, and Blender MCP is available.
5. **Public repo.** Third-party content must be redistributable (CC0 or similar). Paid packs such as Synty can't go into the repo.

## Decision

Treat the generated blockout as the **layout and collision source of truth**, and layer art on top of it in four independent passes. Each pass is data-driven and leaves the blockout untouched.

```
roo2gltf ──► blockout (layout, collision, material slot ids)      [generated, as today]
                │
   pass 1 ──────┤ grd id ──► material map ──► remastered/library   data/environment/materials.json
   pass 2 ──────┤ terrain mesh generated from blockout floors      tools/blender/build_terrain.py
   pass 3 ──────┤ kit-built buildings + props, placed by manifest  art_src/environment/*.blend → data/environment/zone_<rid>.json
   pass 4 ──────┘ lighting, post process, VFX per zone "mood"      DA_ZoneMood assets keyed by rid
```

### 1. Textures: remaster the originals first, replace per texture where they fall short

Every blockout slot is named after its original texture. `data/environment/materials.json` decides, per `grdNNNNN`, which version is used. That lets each texture move up the tiers below on its own, without touching geometry or levels.

| Tier | What | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Placeholder | Lanczos upscale to power-of-two + luminance-derived normal map + roughness by name | `tools/textures/make_placeholders.py` (Pillow only) | **Built (proof of concept)** |
| 1. Remastered original | AI upscale (Real-ESRGAN or similar) → cleanup: remove the painted-in lighting, de-dither → generated normal and ORM maps | Script, plus Krita or Blender touch-up for hero textures | Next |
| 2. AI rework | img2img / ControlNet from the original (allowed now) at low-to-medium denoise: same layout, colours and motifs, more detail. For textures whose upscales look poor (64 px grass and fields, the low-res forest walls) | Local Stable Diffusion/Flux or similar; provenance sidecar | As needed |
| 3. Library material | A tileable stylized master tinted from the original's palette | Material Maker / Blender (see below) | Kit pieces in pass 3 |

**Two kinds of originals need different handling:**
- **Tileable surfaces** (grass, paths, cobble, rock, brick, wood floors, marble) can go to any tier.
- **Painted facades** (`RAZ-BLDG-*`, `raz-DEF-outdr *`, `Raza new building *`, `razmus-*`) are whole building fronts with windows, doors and timber painted on. They carry most of Raza's identity. Tiers 1 and 2 keep them on the blockout walls. When pass 3 rebuilds a building as real geometry, its facade becomes the reference for that building's kit pieces and colours. The kit's own materials are tier 3, tinted to match.

**The tier 3 library**, used by kit pieces and terrain:
- **Author about 30–40 tileable "master" materials** that cover the families the 150 originals fall into. Examples: cobblestone, flagstone, timber frame, plaster, rough stone wall, wood planks, slate and thatch roofs, grass, dirt path, mossy rock, crypt brick, water.
  - Author them procedurally in **Material Maker** (free, MIT, node-based, exports full PBR sets; its `.ptex` graphs are JSON, so they diff in git) or in Blender shader nodes baked by script.
  - Hand-paint touch-ups in Krita or Blender where needed.
- Each master exports **BaseColor (sRGB), Normal (DirectX, green-down, for UE) and ORM (R = AO, G = Roughness, B = Metallic)** at 1024² by default. Hero surfaces get 2048².
  - Target texel density is **about 256–512 px/m**. That's 5–10× the original and plenty for the style.
- A master's tint and variation come from the **dominant palette of the originals it replaces**, sampled by a script from `build/textures/`, so even brand-new materials keep Meridian's colours.
- **Unique pieces** (tapestries, signs, banners, the clock face, stained glass) stay at tier 1 or 2. They're the most recognizable art and don't tile.
- AI-generated textures get a sidecar `<name>.provenance.json` (tool, model, prompt, source texture, date).
- **Style rules for every tier:**
  - Remove or reduce the lighting painted into BaseColor, since real lights now do that job.
  - Medium-to-high roughness.
  - Restrained normal strength: shapes carry the form and normals add surface detail.
  - Texture filtering on, not pixel-art nearest filtering.

#### Proof of concept (2026-10-04): `tools/textures/make_placeholders.py`
- **What it is:** a Pillow-only script, with an optional `--esrgan` hook. It turns all 150 Raza-area originals into `build/textures_placeholder/T_<grd>_D.png` / `T_<grd>_N.png`, plus a `placeholders.json` with masked, roughness and normal-strength values per texture. It takes well under a minute.
- **Base colour:** 4× upscale, rounded to power-of-two sides (capped at 2048) so UE generates mips. The roo2gltf UVs span one texture repeat, so the stretch doesn't change the mapping.
- **Normals:** luminance → blurred height (wrap-around, so tiles stay seamless) → Sobel → DirectX tangent-space normals. The result reads well on cobble, brick and the timber facades: mortar lines, stones and timber beams emboss correctly. It's weaker on flat-painted art such as signs and tapestries, so those get a lower strength. Cut-outs (fences, signs, tree walls) get hard alpha and flat normals in the holes.
- **Gotcha we hit:** Pillow's `ImageFilter.Kernel` flips kernels vertically. The first output was OpenGL-style (green-up). A synthetic raised-square test caught it, and the script now writes DirectX (green-down) as UE expects.
- **UE side:** `tools/ue/environment_materials.py` builds `M_Placeholder` / `M_PlaceholderMasked` (BaseColor × Tint, Normal with a strength lerp, Roughness) and one `MI_<grd>` per texture under `/Game/Generated/Environment`. It assigns them to zone mesh slots by name, with `materials.json` entries taking priority. It's wired into `build_world.py` but **hasn't been validated in-editor yet**. That run was paused so it wouldn't clash with another session.

### 2. Terrain: a generated low-poly mesh, not a UE Landscape

- `tools/blender/build_terrain.py` (headless Blender) reads the blockout's **floor** primitives for outdoor zones and does the following:
  - Re-triangulates them to an even grid of about 1–2 m.
  - Adds a little low-frequency noise away from paths and building footprints, keeping the original heights within ±10 cm wherever the player walks.
  - Writes **vertex-colour splat weights** from each floor polygon's original texture id. For example, R = grass, G = path or cobble, B = dirt, A = rock or mud, blurred across borders so paths get soft edges.
  - Exports one terrain mesh per outdoor zone.
- One UE master material, `M_Terrain`, blends four layers from vertex colour, using world-aligned (triplanar) UVs and a macro-variation texture to break up tiling. Flat-ish shading on large faces keeps the low-poly facet look.
- **The vertical "cliff walls" around the Outskirts** become modular low-poly rock-cliff meshes and dense tree lines placed along the wall segments, as in pass 3. They aren't textured walls.
- **Water** (moat and pond) is a flat plane with a Single Layer Water material.

### 3. Buildings and props: a modular kit, placed by a text manifest

- **Kit, not per-zone sculpting.** Build a Raza kit in `art_src/environment/kits/raza_kit.blend`: wall segments in 1.1 m and 2.2 m widths to match the original grid, corners, timber trims, window and door frames, roof pieces, stairs, crenellations, fences and railings.
  - Start from **CC0 low-poly kits** (candidates to evaluate: Quaternius medieval/nature packs, Kenney Fantasy Town Kit, KayKit medieval sets). Re-texture them with our library so everything shares one look.
  - Model the rest in Blender. Bevel edges and use weighted normals so silhouettes catch light.
- **Per-zone dressing file:** `art_src/environment/zones/<rid>_<name>.blend` links in the generated blockout as a locked reference layer. An artist assembles each building from kit pieces over its blockout footprint.
  - `tools/blender/export_zone_art.py` writes the kit meshes as glTF/FBX plus **`data/environment/zone_<rid>.json`**: a list of `{mesh, transform}` placements plus "hide these blockout faces" rules.
  - Placements are instances of kit pieces, not one merged mesh, so they share memory and LODs and stay editable.
- **Kod-placed objects map automatically:** `data/environment/props.json` maps `class` / `OrnamentalObject.type` → prop mesh (tree, lamp post, brazier, sign, well…). `build_world.py` spawns them at the positions it already has in `zone_layout.json`. Trees replace the sprite trees one-for-one, so the original layout carries over.
- **Collision:** to start, the blockout stays in every zone as a **hidden collision proxy** (not rendered, complex-as-simple, as today), and art meshes have no collision. Gameplay and the smoke test don't change. Later, a zone can switch to art collision, after a check script raycasts the walkable floors and confirms the art matches the blockout within tolerance.
- **Where the files live:**
  - Hand-made `.blend`, `.ptex` and exported textures go in `art_src/environment/` (LFS).
  - Imported UE assets go in `Content/Environment/` (LFS, hand-made rule).
  - Placement manifests and maps go in `data/environment/` (text).
  - Anything a script spawns goes in `/Game/Generated/` (git-ignored).
  - Naming: `SM_`, `M_`/`MI_`, `T_<name>_D|N|ORM`, `NS_` for Niagara.

### 4. Lighting, post process and VFX: per-zone "moods" in data

- **Rendering defaults:** Lumen GI and reflections, Virtual Shadow Maps, and Nanite enabled on kit meshes (instances and LOD-free kits benefit; blockouts keep it off).
- **`UMRZoneMood` DataAsset** keyed by zone rid. It holds the post-process settings (exposure range, grading LUT, bloom, vignette, AO), fog colour and density, sky and sun settings, and ambient particles. The client applies the current zone's mood through the one unbound post-process volume in `L_World` and blends over about 1 s when the zone changes. A volume per sublevel would fight across the pre-loaded neighbour zones.
- **Mood targets:** Raza (warm dusk, light haze), Outskirts (cooler, denser fog in the tree lines), Inn and shops (warm practical lights, low exposure), Crypt and Mausoleum (cold, high contrast, torch-lit).
- **Original sector light levels are a starting hint.** `roo2gltf` can export them, so interiors can start with lights placed where the original was bright.
- **VFX (Niagara):** torch and brazier fire with flicker lights, fountain and water foam, falling leaves and fireflies, dust motes in interiors, and spell effects later (out of scope here). Particles are low-poly or flipbook billboards in the same palette.
- **Not decided here:** a day/night cycle (the original had one). It plugs into the mood asset later.

### Phasing
| Phase | Result | Unblocks |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Tier-0 placeholders with normals on every blockout slot (POC built; in-editor check pending) | Lighting, post-process and VFX tests on real colours with working normals |
| 1 | Tier-1 remastered originals for Raza (AI upscale + cleanup), tier 2 where upscales fail; first library masters + `M_Terrain` | Raza looks "remastered" with blockout shapes |
| 2 | Raza kit + one vertical-slice building (the **Inn**, inside and out) + terrain mesh + trees and lamps from `props.json` | Validates the whole loop: Blender → manifest → generated level |

| 4 | Zone moods, Niagara pass, art-collision switch where it's verified | Final polish |

## Raza look-dev (2026-10-04)

The first pass at making Raza look less flat. These are working tools, not final art; every output is regenerated from the originals plus the text files listed.

### Diagnosis
The flatness came from four places, in order: zero-thickness walls with every window, trim and merlon painted on; one flat ground plane with a noisy upscaled 64 px grass texture; lighting painted into the textures plus a flat default sky; and the Outskirts' 17 m solid "tree walls".

### Facade relief instead of a modular kit (for Raza)
Pass 3 above planned a modular kit assembled over each footprint. For a faithful remaster the painted facades are the identity, so the Hall was rebuilt differently, and that is the approach we'll keep for Raza's buildings:
- `facades.json` records, per original facade texture, where its painted features are, in the texture's own pixels: openings (pointed/round/rect arch, frame width, sill), string-course bands, plinth, crenels. `python tools/environment/facades.py` overlays them on the textures to check the alignment.
- `build_zone_art.py` (headless Blender) takes every blockout face in a building's region (`zone_<rid>.json`). The outward face of a described facade keeps the original texture and UVs, but painted openings are cut and recessed 22–32 cm behind a stone reveal, with the painted glass/door on the panel at the back. Surrounds and bands stand 3–6 cm proud, a plinth runs along the base, and alpha-cut crenellation strips become solid parapets with real merlons. Everything else (roofs, tower, pilasters) is copied. Because the features are found through the wall's own UV mapping, they land exactly where the painting shows them, mirrored and repeated walls included.
- The wall's outer surface stays on the blockout plane, so the hidden collision still matches within the few centimetres of trim.
- Output: `build/environment/zone_<rid>/SM_Z<rid>_<Building>.glb` + `manifest.json` + a `.blend` (art over the remaining blockout) for inspection or hand-editing.
- Kit pieces are still the plan for things the originals didn't paint at all (props, lamp posts, trees) and for hero pieces that need hand modelling (the clock tower's face, the Inn's timber). Those can be added to the same art meshes or placed beside them.

### Nanite displacement (test)
- `make_placeholders.py` writes `T_<grd>_H`. Masonry ("stones" mode: cobble, ashlar, paths, the Hall) can't use luminance as height: the Raza cobble has light mortar, so luminance would raise the mortar. Instead the mortar is found as the minority side of an Otsu threshold, cleaned morphologically, and each stone is domed by a blurred mask. The normal maps are now derived from the same height, so the stones read as rounded instead of the mortar reading as ridges.
- `M_PlaceholderDisplaced` (Material Attributes, because the Python `MaterialProperty` enum has no Displacement pin; `enable_tessellation`; ±2.5 cm at strength 1) is used for zone-art slots only. `materials.json` `displacement` lists the textures that displace and how strongly. `<grd>__glass` and `<grd>__panel` (door leaves) don't.
- Displacement moves vertices along their normals, so flat-shaded corners would crack open. `build_zone_art.py` cuts the art mesh into a 25 cm grid and writes vertex colour R = 0 on corners, openings and open edges (1 inside flat faces); the material multiplies the displacement by it.
- Project: `r.Nanite.AllowTessellation=1` (DefaultEngine.ini).

### All of Raza (second pass)
- **Every building** in `zone_300.json`: Hall, Inn, Tavern, west house, Temple, hut, shop row, two barns, shed, crypt entrance, plus the town wall and the pond. Entries can be limited to textures (`materials`), and a face belongs to the first entry that takes it, so the town wall (by texture, whole-town region) goes last.
- **Openings** for every facade are in `facades.json` (about 30 textures). New feature types: `piers` (the town wall's pilasters), `circle` openings (the Temple's rose window), and per-band `proud_m`.
- **Timber framing through displacement, not geometry.** Measuring every beam and brace would be slow and brittle. `make_placeholders.py` has a "timber" height mode instead: the dark side of an Otsu split (beams, posts, braces) stands proud of the plaster with rounded edges, and Nanite displacement (`materials.json`) turns it into relief. Roof tiles and chimney bricks use the "stones" mode, because their dark lines are gaps.
- **Roofs** (`facades.json` `roofs`): sloped roof faces get a 14 cm slab, a 35 cm eave overhang and 22 cm verge overhangs, with UVs continuing the original mapping.
- **Walls that face nowhere** (both copies fail the outward test, e.g. level floors on both sides) are copied as they are instead of dropped.
- **Cut-out originals copied into the art** (signs, fences) keep their masked material. Crenels rebuilt as solid merlons use a `<grd>__solid` slot.

### Water
The pond is a `kind: "water"` entry. `build_zone_art.py` sinks a shelving bed under the original water floor: 0.2 m at the rim, 0.9 m about 2.5 m in, with the `bed_texture`. It adds skirts from the rim down to the bed and a separate flat water surface (slot `water`, not Nanite) 12 cm above the original floor. `M_Water` is a Single Layer Water material: two world-aligned ripple normals (`T_WaterNormal`, generated) panning in different directions, with absorption and scattering from `materials.json` `water` and Lumen reflections. Collision is unchanged (the original floor), so the pond still reads as wadeable. The fence around it still stops players.

### Light in the shade
- **Ambient floor:** in `moods.json`, `lumen_skylight_leaking` (0.12) keeps occluded corners from going black, and a stronger local exposure (`shadow_contrast_scale` 0.7) lifts deep shade without flattening the sunlit areas.
- **Light sources:** Raza's Kod-placed `Lamp` and `Brazier` objects now render. `data/environment/props.json` maps a class to a kit mesh (`tools/blender/build_prop_kit.py`: iron lamp post with a glazed lantern, tripod brazier with embers) and a warm point light. `build_world.py` spawns them from `zone_layout.json`. Other `OrnamentalObject` types (trees, benches...) can be added to `props.json` the same way once they have meshes.

### Grass wind
Calmer base sway (`wind` 0.3 at `wind_speed` 0.3), scaled between `calm` (0.25) and full by a slowly drifting gust field (macro noise, ~90 m across), so gusts roll across the town now and then. Player-driven grass bending is a later step.

Measured with `run_lookdev.ps1 -Profile` (per camera: everything on / `r.Nanite.Tessellation 0` / grass hidden, 150 frames each, CSV-profiler GPU passes) and summarised with `tools/lookdev/profile_report.py`.

| | GPU ms (12 cameras) |
|---|---|
| Whole frame | 10.8 to 14.6 |
| Nanite tessellation (displacement) | +0.09 to +0.89, mean +0.50 (NaniteVisBuffer 0.42 → 0.87, shadow depths +0.13) |
| Grass scatter (137k instances, shadows on) | +0.40 to +2.52, mean +1.38 (shadow depths +0.9, base pass +0.45, velocities +0.33) |

Asset side: the Raza art meshes total 657k base triangles and 21 MB of uasset. Most of those triangles come from the 25 cm grid that the displacement mask needs. Displacement and the triangle count are not the bottleneck; grass shadows, clouds and TSR are bigger levers.

### Decision: runtime displacement everywhere, Blender overrides where needed (2026-10-04)
We compared three ways of giving the Inn its relief, using the same cameras and profile:

| | Inn mesh | GPU, Inn cameras (ms) | Look |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Runtime Nanite displacement** (chosen) | 31k triangles, ~1 MB | 11.2 to 14.4 | soft relief from the height maps |
| B. Displacement baked into geometry in Blender | 770k triangles, 24 MB | 11.2 to 14.6 (same within noise) | identical to A, with no tessellation |
| C. Blender detail, no displacement: extruded beams traced from the texture, tile-by-tile roof, window boxes | 18k triangles | not finished (stopped) | crisp beams and tiles, flat plaster and stone |

- Runtime displacement covers almost everything at the lowest asset cost and edit effort, so it stays the default for every building (`"displacement": "runtime"`).
- Baking (B) buys nothing at runtime and costs about 20 times the disk space. It stays available (`"displacement": "baked"`) in case tessellation ever has to be switched off.
- **Hybrid for hero buildings:** when a building needs more than relief, an override replaces it. `--seed-override <Building>` writes `art_src/environment/zones/<rid>/<Building>.blend` from the generated mesh; edit that in Blender and the generator uses it from then on. The detail options from C (`"detail": ["timber", "tiles", "window_boxes"]`, `tools/blender/zone_detail.py`) are a starting point for such a building. They're opt-in per building and off by default.

### Texture alignment fix
Several textures sat slightly wrong: two half clocks on the clock tower, fences drawn as 2.8 m bars, and seams where walls were split. The cause was that `roo2gltf` anchored wall textures at world height 0, restarted them at every wall piece, and ignored the per-side offsets and pegging flags. It now follows the original client's rules (`wall_uvs`, see `docs/findings.md`), and floors tile once per grid square like the client. `facades.json` is in texture pixels, so the rebuilt buildings follow automatically.

### Openings left flat (fixed 2026-10-04)
Some painted windows and doors weren't recessed: the middle Temple window, the Cornucopia door, the hut door and some Shops windows. It wasn't displacement. `build_zone_art.py` cuts an opening only into a wall polygon that contains all of it. The original maps often split one wall into several pieces while the painting runs on across the seam, so an opening painted across a seam fitted in neither piece and was silently skipped. That happened to 7 of 72 openings in Raza.

There were two kinds of seam:
- **Seams where the texture continues.** Fixed by joining the pieces into one wall first (`merge_wall_runs`).
- **Mirrored seams.** The second piece shows the texture flipped (`WF_BACKWARDS`), and the opening is centred on the seam: the Temple and two Shops walls. Fixed by keeping one texture mapping per piece on the joined wall, splitting faces and boxes at the seam, and accepting an opening that crosses a mirrored seam when it is centred on it.

To catch this in future, every painted opening on a rebuilt building is now checked off in `build/environment/zone_<rid>/openings.json` (built or not, position, facing, reason). Any opening not built is logged as a WARNING, and `--strict` fails the run. This checks against the facade data itself, which is more reliable than screenshots of every face. The look-dev cameras `tavern_door`, `temple_gable`, `hut_door`, `shops_corner` and `shops_side` show the fixed openings.

### Opening outlines that miss their painting (fixed 2026-10-04)
Two more recesses didn't match the paint, and both were data errors in `facades.json`, not geometry bugs:
- **The Shops tall-wall windows (`grd09658`).** They were rectangles whose top reached into the arch of stones painted above them. They are now a shallow arch (`"shape": "round"` with a low `spring`) under that arch.
- **The Temple rose window (`grd09600` #2).** Its circle sat 10 px above the painted window.

An automatic edge-fitting check was tried and dropped. On these textures, mortar lines, frame edges and leading all score as well as the real outline, so it flagged every opening. Instead, `python tools/environment/facades.py` now also writes `build/environment/facade_check/openings_sheet.png`. It shows all 34 described openings, cropped from their textures, with the opening outline (magenta) and frame (cyan) drawn over them. A misplaced outline is obvious there at a glance. Check it after editing `facades.json`. The look-dev camera `temple_rose` covers the rose window.

### Build speed (2026-10-04)
Every rebuild of the world used to take about 12.5 minutes. It deleted all of `/Game/Generated` (about 200 s, because the editor gathers references for every asset it deletes), then re-imported 450 textures and 32 meshes and rebuilt 13 levels, even when one building had changed.

The build is now incremental, and it can run in the editor you already have open:
- **Inputs are hashed.** `tools/ue/build_cache.py` stores a key for every generated asset: the hash of its source files, its settings from `data/`, the source of the function that builds it, and the keys of the assets it uses.
- **Changed assets are rebuilt in place.** A textures re-import, a master material is emptied and rebuilt, an instance is reset, and a mesh is re-imported over itself. Anything that references them stays valid.
- **Levels change only when their contents do.** A level is rebuilt only when what it places changes, not when a mesh it places is re-imported.
- **Builds can go to the open editor.** `build_world.ps1` sends the build there through Python remote execution (`run_in_editor.py`). It starts a headless editor only when none is open.
- **Blender and texture output can be hashed.** Both write byte-identical files when nothing changed, so content hashes are reliable.

Measured on Raza (RTX 3070):

| Change | Before | Headless | In the open editor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing | ~12.5 min | 38 s (1 s of work) | 3 s |
| One building (Blender 22 s + build) | ~13 min | 22 s + 41 s | 22 s + 15 s |
| A prop light (one level rebuilt) | ~12.5 min | ~45 s (estimated) | 9 s |
| Everything (cold cache, assets exist) | — | 7.3 min of work | — |
| `-Clean` (from scratch) | — | about 9 min (estimated; deletes files before the editor starts) | — |

Textures and look-dev got the same treatment:
- **`make_placeholders.py` is incremental and parallel.** A texture set is remade only when its original, its catalog entry, the rules that apply to it (resolved per texture, so editing one rule remakes only the textures it affects), the options or its code change. Changed sets are made on all cores. Unchanged: 0 s. Everything (`--force`): 22 s, was 96 s. The output is byte-identical to before.
- **Look-dev settles per camera instead of waiting a fixed time.**
  - *Freeze time.* While capturing, world time runs at 1/1000, so clouds, grass wind and water hold still. Lumen, shadows and TSR converge per frame and are unaffected.
  - *Two camera cuts.* Each camera is cut to and sampled every 0.1 s until a coarse brightness grid stops changing. Then it is cut to again: the engine snaps auto exposure to its target on a cut, so exposure comes from the settled lighting. It settles once more and is saved.
  - *Result.* 14 cameras take about 1.3–2.9 s each instead of a fixed 3.5 s. A run takes 44 s (was 72 s), and two runs differ by about as much as two old runs did (mean 1.3/255).
  - *New baseline needed.* Frozen clouds differ from the old drifting ones, so compare new captures with new ones. `-Settle 3` brings back the fixed wait.
- **Look-dev survives a GPU crash.** One capture hit a GPU crash (D3D12 page fault in Nanite virtual-shadow-map drawing) on its second rendered frame, before the tour started. It looks like the same early-frame crash a headless editor had when opening `L_World`. Captures now run `-unattended`, so the crash exits instead of waiting on a dialog, and `run_lookdev.ps1` keeps that log and retries once. The crash itself is still open.

## Options considered

### Textures

#### A. AI-upscale the original textures and derive normal maps (the initial idea; **chosen as the default, tier 1**)
| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Complexity | Low: batch script, plus cleanup on hero textures |
| Cost | Free (local Real-ESRGAN) |
| Quality | Fair to good. Best on 128–256 px art and facades; 64 px dithered art turns to smeared noise; painted-in lighting needs toning down; normals derived from luminance are approximate but convincing on masonry and timber (POC) |
| Faithfulness | Highest: it's the original art |

**Pros:** fastest; keeps the look, as the goal requires; covers all 150 textures in one pass. **Cons:** uneven quality across sources; the normal and ORM maps are inferred, not authored.

#### B. CC0 PBR libraries (Poly Haven, ambientCG)
**Pros:** free, high quality, real normal and roughness data. **Cons:** photoreal, so they clash with low-poly fantasy and with each other. **Kept as:** inputs (for example a rock height map) to the procedural masters, not used directly.

#### C. Procedural / stylized material library + palette sampling (**chosen for kit pieces and terrain, tier 3**)
| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Complexity | Medium: about 30–40 graphs to author once |
| Cost | Free (Material Maker / Blender) |
| Quality | Consistent, any resolution, real normal and ORM maps |
| Faithfulness | Colours and themes only |

**Pros:** one coherent style for new geometry, text-diffable sources. **Cons:** up-front authoring effort, and each master needs an artist's eye.

#### D. AI rework conditioned on the originals (**chosen as a fallback, tier 2**)
**Pros:** adds detail where upscaling can't (64 px sources) while keeping layout, colours and motifs. **Cons:** tileability and consistency vary between runs, so each result has to be reviewed by hand; output must be pinned and recorded (provenance sidecar) to stay reproducible.

### Geometry

#### A. Hand-edit each generated zone mesh in Blender (the initial idea)
**Pros:** simple, direct, no new tooling. **Cons:**
- A `roo2gltf` re-run silently overwrites the work, or the zone forks from its source.
- Each zone becomes one huge unique mesh: no instancing, poor LODs and culling, merge pain in git.
- Collision and walkability risk.

#### B. Fully procedural (Geometry Nodes converts the blockout into buildings)
**Pros:** zero manual work per zone, always regenerable. **Cons:** the generic look takes a lot of effort to get good, and every building needs special cases.

#### C. Blockout as reference + modular kit + text manifest (**chosen**)
**Pros:** regenerable, instanced, artist-controlled, gameplay-safe (collision proxy), and fits ADR 0001's text-first rule. **Cons:** needs an export script and a manifest format; two representations (blockout and art) must be kept in agreement.

#### Terrain: UE Landscape (rejected for now)
Landscape gives painting tools and Landscape Grass. But it's a heightfield that can't reproduce the blockout's sloped sectors and step edges exactly, it's a heavy binary asset per sublevel, it's hard to generate deterministically from our data, and it's smooth rather than faceted. Revisit if later outdoor zones are much bigger than the Outskirts.

## Trade-off analysis

- **Faithfulness vs. quality.** Remastered originals keep the look exactly, but their quality is capped by 64–128 px sources. The per-texture tiers mean we only pay for AI rework or new materials where an upscale visibly fails, and the map makes each choice reversible.
- **Hand-editing vs. regeneration.** Editing the blockout directly feels fastest for the first building. By the third zone it costs more than a kit, and it breaks the generated-levels contract. The kit-plus-manifest route costs one export script up front.
- **Visual collision vs. proxy collision.** A hidden blockout proxy means art can never break movement or the smoke test, at the cost of possible small visual mismatches (feet floating over a beveled step). The ±10 cm rule and a later check script keep that bounded.

## Consequences

- **Easier:** re-running the whole pipeline from a fresh clone; swapping a material everywhere through one map entry; parallel work (materials, kit, terrain and moods don't block each other); keeping original layouts exact.
- **Harder:** we have to keep `materials.json`, `props.json` and the zone manifests in sync with the art; the artist works in Blender against a linked reference rather than editing it freely.
- **Revisit:**
  - The terrain approach for large outdoor zones.
  - Switching collision from proxy to art per zone.
  - Which textures need tier 2 after the first tier-1 pass on Raza.
  - Day/night once the mood asset exists.
